# Humanize pass
*Mandatory after the raw draft. Keep the claims. Kill the AI shape.*

Source stack (from the 2026-09-12 reel):
- [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) v3 (Wikipedia [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing))
- [StoryScope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03136) structural tells, adapted for 15–60s spoken ads

This is not a synonym swap. Do not sprinkle more filler. Do not invent a fact, name, number, quote, or proof point.

---

## When

Every script, every Meta primary text, every human-facing line that leaves this repo. Run it after the raw draft and before save / Trello / Slack.

`brand.md` voice is the writing sample. It overrides pattern defaults. Spoken-word rules in `writing-guide.md` override Wikipedia-style "plain encyclopedia" voice.

---

## How (four steps)

1. **Mark the tells.** Read the whole draft once. Mark strongest first. Watch paragraph shape, not just words. A contrast split across two sentences, three parallel examples, or the same closer after every hook is the same tell at scale.
2. **Rewrite.** Keep every supported claim. Shorten dull parts. Merge or split lines. Change structure. Do not add a fact you do not have.
3. **Check.** Read it aloud. Search for the five tells that most often survive a rewrite: not-X-but-Y, a one-line closer, a dash, a forced triad, a bold label.
4. **Final.** State each point like a person talking to one friend. If a line stays awkward, rewrite the paragraph around its main point. Vary sentence length.

---

## Spoken-word override (do not "fix" these)

Our scripts are read aloud. Wikipedia voice is the wrong target.

**Keep**
- Contractions
- 2–3 mid-sentence humanifiers: "I mean," "you know," "honestly I…," "right?"
- One real self-correction or leftover tension
- One specific messy detail (a real object, time of day, brand-true proof)

**Cut even if they look "human"**
- Standalone staged openers used as scaffolding: "Look." / "Here's the thing." / "Honestly?" / "Real talk." sitting in front of a routine claim
- "Let that sink in." / "Read that again." / "That is the real win."
- Fragment rows: "No setup. No waiting. Just relief."
- Em dashes and en dashes

A humanifier inside a sentence is ordinary. A humanifier as its own beat before every claim is staging.

## A. Staging instead of stating
*One sighting is enough. Act.*

### 1. Not X but Y
"It's not just X, it's Y." / "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." / clipped tails ("…, no guessing").
State the point. Keep a contrast only when the negative half corrects a belief the viewer actually holds.

❌ It's not just a vacuum. It's the backup for when 911 puts you on hold.
✓ When 911 puts you on hold, this is the backup you keep in the kitchen.

### 2. One-line closers and dramatic fragments
A one-sentence paragraph that restates the last point. "That is the real win." A row of fragments. Period-punch words (every. single. day.).
Cut the repeat. Merge fragments into one spoken sentence with a specific claim.

### 3. Sayings that sound deep
"The real question is…" / "at its core" / "what really matters" / "X is not a tool, it's a mirror."
Replace with the specific claim.

### 4. Staged run-up
"Let's dive in." / "Here's what you need to know." / "Look," / "Here's the thing" as a standalone opener.
Remove the run-up, not just its tone. Start on the fact.

### 5. Arguing with no one
"I'm not saying…" / "Don't get me wrong…" / "You might think… but…"
If the objection is real for this buyer, answer it. If you invented the objection to sound balanced, delete it.

---

## B. Rhythm by rule

### 6. Forced triads
Three synonyms pretending to be a complete thought ("innovation, inspiration, and insights"). Keep three only when they are three real things (three proof points, three steps).

### 7. Same opening, every line
"She noted… She noted… She filed…" Merge, change the subject, or start on the action.

### 8. Dashes as the universal connector
No em dashes. No en dashes. No ` -- `. Use a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or rewrite.

### 9. Stacked hedges
"could potentially possibly" / "it might arguably." Keep one real hedge if the claim needs it. Compliance hedges stay.

---

## C. Inflation and borrowed authority

### 10. Overused AI words
Kill on sight in VO: additionally, delve, deep dive, pivotal, crucial, robust (figurative), landscape (abstract), tapestry, testament, underscore, showcase, foster, enhance, vibrant, groundbreaking, nestled, diverse array, game-changing, revolutionary, miracle.

### 11. Inflated significance
"stands as a testament" / "marking a pivotal moment" / "the future looks bright."
Keep the fact. Drop the legacy speech. End on the last concrete fact or the CTA, not a moral.

### 12. Sales-brochure place language
"nestled in" / "breathtaking" / "rich heritage." Say what the thing is.

### 13. Borrowed authority
"experts say" / "studies show" / a prestige-outlet list with no quote.
Use the named source and the actual finding from `brand.md`. Never invent a study.

### 14. Avoiding is / has
"serves as" / "stands as" / "boasts" / "features." Use is, has, does.

---

## D. Leftovers from the chat

### 15. Chatbot residue
"I hope this helps." / "Great question!" / "Would you like…" / "Let me know if…"
Delete the wrapper.

### 16. Knowledge-cutoff guesses
"as of my last update" / "while details are limited" / "she likely grew up…"
State what `brand.md` has, or cut the line. Never guess a proof point.

### 17. Heading restated as the first sentence
If a hook or overlay already said it, the next line must add something new.

## E. StoryScope structural tells (ads, not novels)

Surface words can be cleaned and the script still sounds generated because the *shape* is default. StoryScope: AI drafts over-explain the theme, run one tidy linear track, and land on a resolved moral. Human drafts keep a leftover, a specific, and uneven intensity.

For 15–60s VO, check:

1. **Theme said twice.** If the last line restates the hook as a lesson, cut it. The CTA is an action, not a moral.
2. **Single-track tidy plot.** Hook → problem → mechanism → product → proof → CTA is fine as a skeleton. What fails is every beat explaining the last beat. Skip one "which means…" bridge.
3. **Flat intensity.** If every sentence is the same length and heat, break one, lengthen one, leave one unfinished.
4. **No leftover.** Keep one unresolved bit the viewer already feels (the hold music, the 2am negotiation, the pack they still have not opened). Do not wrap it in a bow.
5. **Generic theme, no object.** "It's about peace of mind" is a theme. "I keep it on the highest kitchen shelf" is a human beat. Prefer the object.
6. **Protagonist as slogan.** The speaker should sound like they chose something, not like they are reciting the brand's about page.

Do not add flashbacks or moral ambiguity that the brand cannot own. Do not invent a messy detail. Use one that is already true.

## After-pass checklist

- [ ] No not-X-but-Y unless the viewer actually believes X
- [ ] No one-line closer that repeats
- [ ] No em dash
- [ ] No forced triad of synonyms
- [ ] No staged "Look / Here's the thing / Honestly?" opener
- [ ] No AI word from §10
- [ ] No invented proof
- [ ] 2–3 mid-sentence humanifiers, not one on every line
- [ ] CTA is an action, not a moral
- [ ] Read aloud: if it sounds like a product page or a LinkedIn post, rewrite
